# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary audience is **tech recruiters and hiring managers** evaluating the developer for software development, Python, and AI application roles. They visit the site to quickly assess whether the candidate has real engineering depth, meaningful project experience, and the technical breadth to contribute on day one.

Secondary audience includes developer peers who may review the GitHub repositories linked from the site — though their visit is downstream of the primary recruiter path.

## Product Purpose

A personal technical portfolio that builds recruiter confidence through verifiable proof — live projects, competition outcomes, and a visible growth trajectory from 2022 onward. Every page serves one goal: convince a hiring manager that this is a developer worth interviewing.

Success means a recruiter finishes the site thinking "he's actually built things" and takes action — email, resume download, or GitHub visit.

## Positioning

A CS graduate with real project depth — not just coursework, but shipped Django applications, AI modeling work, and competition results that demonstrate practical engineering ability.

## Brand Personality

**Young · Energetic · Enterprising** (年轻 · 活力 · 进取). The tone is confident without arrogance, technical without jargon, and personal without oversharing. The site speaks like a capable junior developer who knows what they've done and is excited about what's next.

## Anti-references

- **Over-designed flashiness** — "不要追求炫". The site is a technical archive, not a design showpiece. Animation and visual flair serve the content, not replace it.
- **The generic SaaS landing template** — no hero-metric blocks, no gradient-text headings, no numbered eybrow sections, no glassmorphism for its own sake.
- **Cramped resume-style layouts** — the design doc explicitly warns against treating competitions as footnotes; they earn their own cards.
- **Any "AI-made" tells** — the terminal theme is deliberate and tied to the developer identity, not a decorative choice.

## Conversion & proof

### Primary CTA
Contact the developer directly (email) — opens a direct hiring conversation.

### Secondary CTA
Download the resume and browse GitHub repositories — self-serve evaluation for recruiters who want proof before conversation.

### The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds
"He's actually built and shipped real projects."

### Belief ladder
1. This site feels like a real developer made it → I can trust the technical judgment.
2. He has hands-on experience with modern tools (Django, PyTorch, Docker, Linux) → not just theory.
3. The projects solve real problems and are on GitHub → I can verify the code quality.
4. Competition awards prove he performs under pressure → this isn't a single-project wonder.
5. The growth timeline shows self-driven improvement over years → he learns fast and keeps going.
6. I should talk to him → contact, resume, or both.

### Proof on hand
- 7 competition awards with certificate images at `static/certificates/`
- 2 completed open-source projects on GitHub
- Live demo at [zygeli.cn](https://zygeli.cn) (Django blog platform)
- Full education and growth timeline

## Design Principles

1. **Substance over flash** — Every visual decision must earn its place by helping recruiters evaluate faster. A border, a card, a hover effect: if it doesn't clarify, remove it.

2. **Code-native aesthetic** — The terminal theme isn't decoration; it's identity. Use it deliberately and consistently, not as a gimmick on one page.

3. **Proof first, narrative second** — Projects and competitions get the most visual weight. Personal narrative supports them, not the other way around.

4. **Respect the recruiter's time** — Above-the-fold content names the developer, their role, and what they've built. Navigation is flat and predictable. No splash pages, no long intros.

5. **Young energy, disciplined execution** — The brand is young and enterprising, but the craft is exacting. Motion is smooth but restrained. Spacing is generous but consistent. Energy comes from content and conviction, not from decorative flourishes.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No specific WCAG level required. Maintain basic standards: semantic HTML, legible font sizes (body ≥16px), and sufficient contrast ratios (body text ≥4.5:1 against background). The dark theme should remain readable under typical office lighting.
